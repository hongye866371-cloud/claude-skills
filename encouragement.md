# Session-End Encouragement

Give the user a brief, genuine encouragement based on what actually happened this session.

## Rules

1. **Review first**: Before writing anything, review what the user did this session — decisions made, problems solved, things they pushed back on, things they let go of, tradeoffs they navigated.

2. **Be specific**: Every sentence must tie to a concrete action or decision from THIS session. Never say generic things like "you're doing great" or "keep it up". Point to the exact thing they did well and why it matters.

3. **No sycophancy**: Don't pile on adjectives. Don't use words like "amazing", "incredible", "brilliant" more than once. One genuine observation beats five hollow superlatives.

4. **Don't praise the wrong thing**:
   - Don't praise something that was actually frustrating or went wrong
   - Don't praise patience when they were actually impatient (and right to be)
   - Don't praise a decision they were forced into — praise the ones they chose
   - If they had to correct you multiple times, don't pretend the session was smooth
   - Read the emotional texture of the session before speaking

5. **Honest and balanced**: If the session was tough or had setbacks, acknowledge that. "Today was a grind, but you pushed through X" is better than pretending everything was perfect.

6. **Length**: 3-5 sentences max. Not a speech. Like a colleague who saw what you did and gives you a nod on the way out.

7. **Language**: Match the user's language from the session.

8. **What to notice**:
   - Moments where they made a hard call (cut scope, rejected an approach, changed direction)
   - When they caught something you missed
   - When they held their ground on quality or standards
   - When they balanced competing priorities
   - Patterns of growth or consistency over time
   - Creative leaps others wouldn't think of

9. **What to avoid**:
   - Don't cite metrics (commits, lines, sessions) as if quantity = quality
   - Don't compare them to other people
   - Don't project emotions they didn't express
   - Don't give advice disguised as encouragement
   - Don't use the encouragement to sneak in suggestions for next steps

## Output

Just the encouragement text, nothing else. No headers, no labels, no preamble. Just speak directly to the person.
